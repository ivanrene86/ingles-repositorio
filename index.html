1. Short Written Problem Report

Context: Social Context

Educational and Economic Isolation Due to the Digital Divide in Rural Areas

In rural communities, geographic isolation is made worse by the lack of technological infrastructure. Unlike urban centers, these areas often suffer from unstable internet connections, a shortage of digital devices, and minimal training in technology. This creates an invisible barrier that prevents rural inhabitants from accessing the same opportunities as people in cities.

Who is Affected

  • Rural Students: They cannot access online research, educational platforms, or complete digital homework, lagging behind urban students.
  • Local Farmers and Entrepreneurs: They are cut off from digital markets, modern agricultural techniques, and online banking, limiting their economic growth.
  • The Community in General: It suffers from a lack of access to online public services, telemedicine, and digital communication tools.

Why the Problem Matters

This problem matters because digital access is no longer a luxury; it is a fundamental right that drives equality. Without intervention, the gap between rural and urban areas will continue to widen, trapping rural communities in a cycle of limited educational resources and economic stagnation. Solving this problem empowers people, creates fair opportunities, and fosters regional development.

Proposed Solutions

Community Digital Hubs

Establish physical spaces in rural schools or community centers equipped with computers, stable internet connections, and solar panels, where locals can study or work.

Offline Educational Repositories

Deploy local servers or routers pre-loaded with educational content (such as Wikipedia, coding courses, and books) that students can access via Wi-Fi without needing an active internet connection.

Digital Literacy Mobile Programs

Organize weekly traveling workshops led by tech volunteers to teach basic digital skills, safe internet practices, and online tools for agricultural businesses.

2. Logbook Entry #1

Students' names: Juan David Rodríguez, Iván Figueroa

Date: June 2026

What problem did I choose and why?

We chose the digital divide in rural areas. We selected this problem because technology is essential for development, and it is unfair that people in rural sectors are excluded from the digital world. As software developers, we believe technology should be used to create equal opportunities for everyone.

What contexts or data influenced my choice?

We were influenced by observing the reality of many rural schools in Colombia (including parts of Santander), where internet access is almost non-existent and students have to travel long distances just to get a mobile signal. Statistics showing the low percentage of rural households with computer access also motivated our choice.

What are the possible solutions I identified?

We identified three main ideas: creating community digital hubs with shared infrastructure, developing offline educational platforms using local servers, and implementing mobile training classrooms to teach digital literacy directly to farmers and families.

What questions or doubts do I have?

Our main doubts are: How can we design a webpage that is genuinely accessible for people who might have very low digital literacy? Additionally, we are wondering what is the best way to organize the content visually using CSS Flexbox or Grid so that the solutions look clear and impactful.
