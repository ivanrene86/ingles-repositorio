1. Short Written Report on the Problem

Context: Social Context

Educational and Economic Isolation Due to the Digital Divide in Rural Areas

In rural communities, geographical isolation is worsened by the lack of technological infrastructure. Unlike urban centers, these areas often suffer from unstable internet connections, a shortage of digital devices, and minimal tech training. This creates an invisible barrier that prevents rural residents from accessing the same opportunities available to people in cities.

Who is Affected

  • Rural Students: They cannot access online research or educational platforms, nor can they complete digital homework, leaving them lagging behind compared to urban students.
  • Local Farmers and Entrepreneurs: They remain disconnected from digital markets, modern agricultural techniques, and online banking, which limits their economic growth.
  • The Community at Large: Suffers from a lack of access to online public services, telemedicine, and digital communication tools.

Why the Problem Matters

This problem matters because digital access is no longer a luxury; it is a fundamental right that drives equality. Without intervention, the gap between rural and urban areas will continue to grow, trapping rural communities in a cycle of limited educational resources and economic stagnation. Solving this issue empowers people, creates fair opportunities, and fosters regional development.

Proposed Solutions

Community Digital Centers

Establish physical spaces in rural schools or community halls equipped with computers, a stable internet connection, and solar panels, where local residents can study or work.

Offline Educational Repositories (No Connection)

Implement local servers or pre-loaded routers with educational content (such as Wikipedia, programming courses, and books) that students can access via Wi-Fi without needing an active internet connection.

Mobile Digital Literacy Programs

Organize weekly traveling workshops led by tech volunteers to teach basic digital skills, safe internet practices, and online tools for agricultural businesses.

2. Project Logbooks

Student Names: Juan David Rodriguez, Ivan Figueroa

Date of Update: June 2026

Logbook Entry #1 (Moment 1)

What problem did I choose and why?

We chose the digital divide in rural areas. We selected this problem because technology is essential for development, and it is unfair that people in rural sectors are excluded from the digital world. As software developers, we believe technology should be used to create equal opportunities for everyone.

What contexts or data influenced my choice?

We were influenced by observing the reality of many rural schools in Colombia (including parts of Santander), where internet access is almost non-existent and students have to travel long distances just to get a cell phone signal. Statistics showing the low percentage of rural households with access to computers also motivated our choice.

What are the possible solutions I identified?

We identified three main ideas: creating community digital centers with shared infrastructure, developing offline educational platforms using local servers, and implementing mobile training classrooms to teach digital literacy directly to farmers and families.

What questions or doubts do I have?

Our main doubts are: How can we design a webpage that is truly accessible for people who might have a very low level of digital literacy? Additionally, we wonder what the best way is to organize the content visually using CSS Flexbox or Grid so that the solutions look clear and impactful.

Logbook Entry #2 (Moment 2)

What HTML structure did I design and why?

We designed a completely semantic structure based on accessibility guidelines. We used <header> and <nav> for the main heading and global navigation. For the page core, we implemented a <main> block composed of independent sections identified by IDs. Each section divides its specific content using <article> elements, ensuring information is modular, easy to read for assistive technologies, and features a clean heading hierarchy (h1, h2, h3).

What CSS strategies am I planning (Flexbox, Grid, colors, spacing)?

Flexbox: We implemented it to align the navigation bar elements horizontally and flexibly, and within the contact form to achieve a clean vertical column layout.
Grid: We applied it in the solutions section to create an intelligent, dynamic layout of independent cards that adapt automatically.
Colors: We used a palette based on Forest Green (#2e6f40), Dark Green (#1e4620), and Slate Grey (#2d3748) to convey an organic identity.
Spacing: Standardized margins of 40px were applied between sections along with internal padding in cards to improve legibility.

What challenges emerged?

The greatest challenge was making the solutions grid completely responsive without writing dozens of lines of redundant code. We solved this by utilizing the auto-fit and minmax properties of CSS Grid, which allowed the cards to rearrange themselves seamlessly when shifting from desktop viewports to mobile phone screens.

What comments or feedback did I receive after the socialization?

During the socialization session with our peers and instructor, they highlighted the effective use of color contrast to support rural accessibility. Additionally, they suggested that for Phase 3 we should incorporate subtle transitions or animations on the interactive elements and buttons to enhance user experience.
